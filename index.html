Information security assessment for an independent K-12 school

A full review of cloud, on-premises hardware and software services, calibrated to a school's real risks, then turned into a 12-month roadmap for the board.

How the engagement ran

  1. DiscoverReviewed the school's technical documentation with the IT team: users, devices, network, servers, systems and hosting.
  2. AssessRan a 48-question control questionnaire across 10 domains. Every answer got a red, yellow or green rating.
  3. CalibrateReviewed the IT team's corrective actions and re-rated findings against a scale written for a school.
  4. PlanWrote the diagnosis, the technical proposal and the action plan with owners, priorities and time frames.
  5. PresentTook the plan to the board with four decisions to approve.

Deliverables
Gap analysis register, corrective-action memo, diagnostic deck, technology plan report, board deck.

Calibrating risk to a school

Corporate benchmarks do not fit a school with non-technical users, mixed-ownership devices and a small IT team. I wrote a rating scale for the school, then re-rated each finding against the IT team's evidence.

Critical

Exposure of minors' data, uncontrolled remote access, or total loss of backups.

Accepted with conditions

Acceptable when documented and reviewed every year.

Controlled

Control in place and evidenced.

Proportionate by design
  • Strong authentication scoped by how sensitive a role's data is, not rolled out to everyone at once
  • Sharing restrictions tied to classification labels, not a global block that stops daily work
  • No central management of students' personal devices: operational and legal limits
  • Physical controls sized to the institution, with periodic access reviews
Govern before buying
  • Use the firewall, cloud admin console and directory policies the school already owns
  • Most priority actions fit inside existing licences
  • Lightweight evidence: a one-page incident checklist, an update register, a logged yearly restore test
  • Accepted risks written down and reviewed yearly with leadership

Prioritisation criteria
  • Cut critical security and data risk first
  • Remove high-impact friction for teachers and administration
  • Reuse infrastructure and tools already owned
  • Favour what integrates, scales and the team can sustain
What the board was asked to decide
  • Start of the security actions and the immediate usability fixes
  • Selection criteria for admissions: extend, buy or build
  • Design of the central data architecture
  • An internal owner per workstream and a preliminary budget for specialists

How it transfers to higher education
